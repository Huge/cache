Back to the “global village”, with an AI helper

Imagine a society that isn’t run by wallets. In their place: a memory of what people have done.

The old problem: Back when people lived in small villages of about 50, they didn’t need money. When the blacksmith fixed the baker’s roof, the baker remembered. Later, when the blacksmith needed bread, he got it, because the baker knew: “This one is useful, we owe each other.” It all ran on trust and reputation.

But once we started trading with strangers on the other side of the world, that system broke down. Nobody can remember the good deeds of a million people. So we invented money. Money is really just “liquid trust”. It is anonymous proof that at some point you did something useful, which is why you can buy a bread roll today without the shop assistant knowing who you are.

The problem with money: Money is “dumb”. It doesn’t remember whether you got it through honest work, theft or an inheritance. It wipes out all the context. All that’s left is a number.

A new solution (a shared attribution graph): The idea is to use technology to go back to that “village” system, but at global scale. Instead of sending each other money, the system would remember who did what, and for whom. Picture a giant digital web of gratitude and credit:

  • You write a great article → the system records that it helped a thousand people.
  • You help your neighbour move house → the system records a link of mutual help.

The role of personal AI (taking away the mental load): You object: “That’s crazy. I can’t track a billion transactions and judge whether someone is a good person just to sell them a coffee!”

And that is exactly where personal AI comes in. Your personal AI assistant works like the best-informed gossip in the village. It knows everyone. When a stranger comes to you asking for something, your AI checks this “digital web” in a split second and whispers in your earpiece:

“You can trust this person. They don’t have any ‘money’, but last week they helped build a school and their community thinks highly of them. Give them the coffee. The system will record that you helped someone useful, and your reputation goes up too.”

The result: An economy that isn’t built on piling up numbers in an account (which rewards greed), but on provable benefit to others. There’s no need to convert everything into dollars or euros any more, because the AI does the hard sum of “who deserves what” for us.
